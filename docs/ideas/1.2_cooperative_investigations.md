# Idea for 1.2: cooperative investigations and base camp

Noted on 2026-11-11, at Romain's request. None of this has started: it is a lead for version 1.2, not to be mixed up
with 1.1.

## Romain's idea

Investigations with several players should play the cooperation card, instead of being a regular investigation that
several players do side by side (which is what they are in 1.1).

- **Bigger investigations**, for **alpha** Pokémon.
- **A base camp** to set up. Roles are split: one does the analyses, another takes readings in the field, others fight
  Pokémon.
- **A cooperation system**: one sends information while the other writes it down, and everyone's information is
  cross-checked.
- **The field tent**: an item you place, and a tent appears with all the equipment needed. Analyses are done there. Once
  the Pokémon can be tracked, the tent is taken down and everyone sets off after it.

Romain's clarification (2026-11-11): the tent will probably come with quite a few tools. So it is not just a shelter:
it is what brings the equipment, and the list of those tools is still to be defined.

## What already exists in 1.1 to build on

- Case file shared between a lead investigator and their teammates (`CaseFile.leader`, `CaseFile.team`), invitations
  from the kit's Team tab, loot and experience for each player.
- Counter of cases solved together, per pair of players (`Profile.partners`).
- Special investigation kinds (`CaseFile.special`) with their rules, loot and mini-games: an "alpha" investigation could
  be a new one, reserved for teams.
- The kit's pocket sequencer: in 1.0, a sequencer block and a board block existed, then were removed at Romain's request
  so that everything fits in the kit. The tent would bring back a fixed place, but a temporary one that can be taken
  down.

## 1.1 playtest feedback and Romain's decisions (2026-11-12)

Finding on 1.1 co-op: with five traces, playing with several players does not make the investigation harder, just
faster. And since there is only one Pokémon, only one player catches it at the end.

Decided:
- **Expeditions are for groups only** (no solo version where one player takes several roles).
- **A single alpha Pokémon**, fought together. Guards around the alpha are set aside: we do not see how to catch them or
  balance them without it being too strong.
- Once the alpha is beaten, **every player may try to catch it**, with a real chance of failure. The alpha is strong
  (good IVs) without everything being maxed out.
- Flow: pick an expedition → form the team → receive the tent and equipment → travel to a place that may be far away →
  pitch the tent → the board gives the starting information → everyone takes a role on the board.
- **Clues that complement each other**, more varied than plain traces. Some can only be detected if two players are in
  two different places at the same time.
- **Scout ↔ researcher back-and-forth**: a clue brought back to camp and analysed gives a new check to do in the field.
- **Two-player analyses**: press at the same moment, each at their station, and you can see who missed.
- **More work than solo**: an expedition is long and hard, with quality rewards at the end.
- Once the Pokémon is identified, the game offers to **take down the tent** and go after it with the kit. The hunt must
  be **much harder than in 1.1**: cries alone, taken in turns, locate it too fast.

Romain's clarifications (2026-11-12, second pass):
- **No shared battle.** The alpha is beaten, then **players try to catch it in turn, one attempt each**, in order of
  contribution during the expedition (whoever worked the most goes first). Catch rate set by the mod. If everyone
  fails, the alpha flees but **the expedition counts as a success**. Two options for the gesture: an animation of the
  mod's own (no Poké Ball), or the real Poké Ball, but done properly.
- Alpha: 3 random perfect IVs, the others random, hidden ability one time in two. Approved.
- Roles kept: **researcher** (analyses, board, leads to check), **scouts** (field), **cartographer** (cross-checks the
  bearings). The researcher must never wait: long analyses, things to craft, real progress while the scouts search.
- **No trainer role**: wild Pokémon attack the players who are searching, and everyone defends themselves.
- Clues: footprints, scratches and samples already exist. To add: meal leftovers, paired clues, echo (two scouts
  listening at the same time), night clues, back-and-forth with the researcher.
- **Each two-player mini-game is designed on its own before being coded** (several types, in the spirit of Keep
  Talking and Nobody Explodes). Nothing gets coded until how it works is approved.
- Hunt: decoys = cries of other random Pokémon; fresh signs that fade; the alpha moves; final approach by surrounding
  it, with a map that guides ("well placed", "further east").
- The expedition is played **over a large area**: the board says where to search by biome ("in the swamp to the
  south-east"), so there has to be some logic for which biome is where, and everyone heads to their zone with their kit.
- We move forward **step by step**, starting with how an expedition starts.

Starting an expedition, approved by Romain (2026-11-12):
1. The board has two tabs, Investigations and Expeditions. Two mission orders at a time, renewed once per in-game day:
   region and direction ("Swamp, about 1800 blocks south-east"), 2 to 4 players, difficulty, estimated duration, vague
   hint about the target.
2. Whoever picks it becomes the leader. The Team tab can invite any player on the server; each member has a "Ready"
   button.
3. With at least 2 players ready, the leader starts the departure: they receive the folded tent, and everyone gets a
   compass pointing to the region.
4. Travel without teleporting. In the region (about 150 blocks from the centre), the kit offers to set up camp.
5. Tent pitched on fairly flat ground, with at least two members within 30 blocks: the camp board opens the briefing.
Points still open, with the default proposal: required rank (to be decided), solo investigation paused during the
expedition, quitting without penalty before the tent is pitched, tent handed over if the leader disconnects, one
expedition at a time with a cooldown between two.

Gathering and analysis phase, Romain's feedback (2026-11-12):
- The researcher must never wait for the others: they are the one who **sets up the camp** and installs the stations
  (to be detailed).
- The cartographer's **relay beacons** are approved: you drop your satchel there, and it arrives at the tent.
- With 2 players, the scout is also the cartographer.
- The co-op mini-games are detailed one by one, starting with the footprint description.
- Communication: plan for it **with and without Simple Voice Chat**. Without it, players talk through the radio with
  written messages (bubbles that appear on the radio). Simple Voice Chat is an optional dependency, never required.
- With Simple Voice Chat: players in the field talk to each other directly, but the player at the tent hears their
  voices **coming out of the radio set** (sound positioned on the block), and talks to the others by standing near the
  radio. Technical lead: `MicrophonePacketEvent` + `createStaticAudioChannel` / `createLocationalAudioChannel` from
  the Simple Voice Chat API, to be confirmed with a prototype.
- Footprint description: settings (90 s, one mistake allowed, a single defect reused during the hunt) to be tested as
  they are. Cobblemon 1.8.4 has no footprints, and those of the official games cannot be redistributed: idea of
  footprints **generated from the species data** (egg groups, weight, height, types), always the same for a given
  species.
- **Hold-to-talk radio button** (push-to-talk), on the kit for players in the field and on the tent's radio set for the
  researcher: only the voice sent while the button is held goes through the radio.
- Radio guidance, validated in principle (2026-11-12): an analysis gives the researcher a **concentration map** of a
  zone about 40 × 40 blocks, scrambled with false hot spots; a cache is buried there. The scout sees nothing (radar
  scrambled) and follows the researcher's voice; the researcher sees the scout's position live (and the scout's
  heading, except at high difficulty, where it stays hidden). The scout has a limited number of **readings** that
  sharpen the map around them, and the scent **fades** in about 5 minutes (both at once). Digging commits: two wrong
  digs and the cache is lost. The cache may also give a **bait** for the hunt (a link to look into). Difficulty: 2 to 4
  false spots, 5 to 3 readings. Written mode: arrows, "Stop" and "Dig" quick replies.
- Paired clue, validated (2026-11-12): two linked points 30 to 80 blocks apart, one scout at each, two variants kept.
  **Two-exit burrow**: A holds the exit (gauge to keep in a zone against irregular pushes), B samples inside; if A
  lets go, B loses part of the progress. **Double lock**: each scout sees the other's code and dictates it by radio,
  then both confirm within a 1 s window; the screen shows who was early or late. Failure: 30 s wait, and the clue
  closes for 5 minutes after 3 failures. Success counts double; contribution split half and half. Difficulty: window
  1 s then 0.5 s, codes of 3 then 5 symbols.
- Four-handed analysis, validated (2026-11-12): some samples are **unstable**, cannot go through relay beacons and
  must be carried to the tent, then analysed by two players at a double bench. Post A (heating) sees the real
  temperature, which drifts, but not the target; post B (protocol) reads the steps and adds the reagents, but does not
  see the temperature. Failure names the post ("Overheating, post A", "Wrong reagent, post B"); first mistake loses
  part of the sample, second ruins it. Success gives the best analysis result of the expedition. Difficulty: 3 to 6
  steps, drift, range width. Open: other protocols later (e.g. a two-player centrifuge).
- The echo (two scouts listening at the same time) moves to the hunt phase.
- **At 2 players, all roles merge**: there are no fixed roles and each player does whatever is needed (field, lab,
  map). Consequence to keep in mind: the lab must keep running while nobody is at the tent.

Checked in Cobblemon 1.8.4 (not tested in game yet): there is a `GEN_9_MULTI` battle format (two against two) and a
generic battle start (`BattleRegistry.startBattle`); the `PokemonCatchRateEvent` and `PokeBallCaptureCalculatedEvent`
events let the catch chance be adjusted; a Pokémon has a native `isAlpha` flag (alpha mark and particles).

## Full proposal, from the camp to the capture (2026-11-12, to be reviewed by Romain)

Romain asked for a complete proposal for everything left, to review in one go. Nothing below is decided.

### 0. Overall timing

| Step | Target length |
| --- | --- |
| Team, departure, journey | 5 to 10 min |
| Camp and collection / analysis | 30 to 45 min |
| Hunt | 20 to 30 min |
| Approach, battle, capture | 5 to 10 min |
| **Total** | **60 to 90 min** |

Zones to explore grow with the team: 3 zones with 2 players, 4 with 3, 5 with 4. Each zone holds 3 or 4 clues, at
least one of them a two-player mini-game. The state is saved: an expedition can span several sessions.

### 1. The camp (researcher)

**Setting up.** The folded tent unfolds into the tent plus **equipment crates**. Each station comes out of its crate
through a short assembly mini-game (20 to 40 s) and is placed freely around the tent:

| Station | Role | Need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board | Zones, verification requests, suspects, contributions, map | none |
| Radio set | Talking to the field (written or voice), push-to-talk | none |
| Field workbench | Crafts vials, reagents, baits | local materials |
| Generator | Powers the analysis stations | wood, charcoal or coal |
| Sequencer, microscope, centrifuge | Analyses (lab mini-game, then waiting) | power; light for the microscope |
| Cooler | Keeps samples from going bad | ice or snow (more in hot biomes) |
| Double bench | Four-handed analysis | power |
| Relay receiver | Receives the bags sent from beacons, sends vials back | none |

Where the camp goes is a choice: water within 8 blocks for the reagents, wood nearby for the generator, snow or ice
if it is hot.

**Running the lab.** Each analysis station has a queue; an analysis = the existing lab mini-game, then 2 to 5 minutes
of waiting, then the result is read on the board. Analyses keep running when nobody is at the tent (needed with merged
roles at 2 players). Each analysis uses reagents made at the workbench from local materials, some of them specific to
the zone biomes (marsh plants, mushrooms, clay...), which scouts send back through the beacons. Vials go the other way:
crafted by the researcher, sent to the scouts through the relays.

**Upkeep.** About every 8 to 10 minutes, one camp event: generator low on fuel, storm (repair the tent with a short
mini-game, rain shortens some timers), cooler melting, a wild Pokémon raiding the sample crate (chase it off or fight it).

**Camp upgrades during the expedition**, paid with rare materials brought back from the zones: a third analysis slot,
faster waiting (−30 %), a better radio (scouts' positions on the board map), a generator feeder. They last for the
expedition only. This is the researcher's progression while the others are away.

### 2. Collection, in short

Scouts head to the zones shown on the board, find clues with the radar, sample them with the existing field
mini-games (bag of 4 samples), drop the bag at a relay beacon or carry unstable samples by hand. The five co-op
mini-games (footprint description, radio guidance, paired clue, four-handed analysis, reverse comparison) are spread
across the zones. Analysis results remove suspects or create **verification requests** that appear on site only when
a scout goes there.

**Ambushes.** Taking a sample has a chance to make a nearby wild Pokémon charge the player and force a wild battle
(normal wild Pokémon, levels scaled on the team, catchable as usual, fleeing allowed). Everyone defends themself: there
is no fighter role.

**Identification.** When the board leaves few suspects, any player at the board can propose the identification. Wrong:
the suspect is struck out, one new verification request appears, and the expedition rating drops one step. Right: the
board offers **"Pack up the camp"**.

### 3. Packing up and the bivouac

Packing up turns the tent back into an item in a few seconds; camp upgrades are lost. During the hunt the tent can be
pitched again as a **bivouac**: only the board (map) and the radio set, no lab. It is the cartographer's base.

### 4. The hunt

The alpha appears out of sight 300 to 600 blocks from the camp, in a biome that suits it. It moves between **points of
interest** (the roost found in the reverse comparison, water, feeding spots) by 40 to 80 blocks every 3 to 4 minutes,
and goes back to its roost at night.

- **Echo and cross-referencing.** A single listening gives a bearing with a wide margin (±20°): useless alone. Two
  listenings within 5 s of each other, from points at least 40 blocks apart, give an intersection zone shown **only on
  the cartographer's map** (bivouac board or the cartographer's kit; with 2 players, whoever holds the map). 30 s
  cooldown per player.
- **Decoys.** Other random Pokémon cries ring out around the area. A listening may lock onto a decoy; the cry heard
  can be replayed, and a player who knows the target's cry can throw the bearing away. Nothing tells them for sure.
- **Fresh signs.** The alpha leaves signs as it moves (broken branches, trampled grass, footprints). They fade after
  about 3 minutes and give its direction of travel. A footprint with the **single defect** from the footprint
  description confirms it is the alpha and not an ordinary member of the species nearby.
- **Bait.** The bait found in a radio guidance cache can be placed: within a few minutes the alpha comes to it and
  stays about 2 minutes, which makes the approach easier.

### 5. Approach and encirclement

Within 60 blocks the alpha becomes visible and **on the lookout**: a wariness gauge rises when a player sprints,
rides, makes noise or stands upwind (wind direction shown on the kit). Full gauge: it flees 100 to 150 blocks and part
of the hunt starts over.

**Encirclement.** The kit shows a ring 15 to 30 blocks around the alpha split into sectors, one per player (2 players:
opposite sides; 3: every 120°; 4: every 90°). Each player gets a guidance line: "Well placed", "Further east", "Too
close". When everyone is in place, "Surrounded!" and a closing countdown: everyone moves in, sneaking, down to about
8 blocks. If someone breaks formation, the alpha bolts **through the biggest gap**.

### 6. The battle

No shared battle (decided). Proposal: the team picks a **champion** (by default, the top contributor) who battles the
alpha alone, while the others hold the encirclement; if a gap opens during the battle, the alpha can flee at the end
of it. Alternative to discuss: each in turn, with the alpha keeping its HP between battles.

Technical point to prototype: a wild Pokémon that reaches 0 HP faints and disappears. The mod must catch that moment
and keep the alpha on the spot, **exhausted** (1 HP, no AI), for the capture.

### 7. The capture

- **Order**: by contribution score, computed over the whole expedition (clues, analyses, co-op successes, camp work,
  hunt actions), shown on the kit before the capture.
- **Gesture**: the real Poké Ball, done properly. Only the player whose turn it is can throw; any other ball bounces
  off ("not your turn"). One throw each, 30 s to throw before the turn passes.
- **Chance**: set by the mod (`PokeBallCaptureCalculatedEvent`), for example 25 % base, up to +10 % from contribution,
  a small bonus from better balls; a Master Ball counts as a plain ball.
- **Failure**: the alpha breaks free and roars, next player. After the last one, it flees (run then particles and
  despawn). **The expedition still counts as a success.**
- **The alpha caught**: Cobblemon alpha flag, 3 perfect IVs at random, the others random, hidden ability one time in
  two, level a little above the team's.

### 8. Rewards

- **Everyone present**: large investigator XP, a reward chest whose tier depends on the expedition rating (time,
  failures, wrong identifications), rare Cobblemon items (mints and the like), the footprint added to the footprint
  logbook, and an **expedition plaque** (a block naming the species and the team) to put up at home.
- **By contribution**: a bonus on top for the most active; a player with almost no contribution gets a reduced share
  and throws last.
- **Pace**: one expedition at a time per player, with a server-configurable delay between two.

### 9. Disconnections and server restarts

- Timers that pressure the team (fading scent, fresh signs, alpha moves) pause when fewer than 2 members are online.
- The tent and the leader role pass to another member if the leader stays offline.
- Everything is saved in the world, like the 1.1 state.

### 10. Suggested build order (once the design is approved)

1. Start: expedition orders on the board, team and "Ready", departure, folded tent, journey, pitching the tent,
   written radio.
2. Camp: stations, needs, lab queues, upkeep, upgrades, relay beacons.
3. The five co-op mini-games, one by one, each tested in game before the next.
4. Hunt: alpha movement, echo, decoys, fresh signs, bait, bivouac.
5. Approach, encirclement, battle, exhausted alpha, capture in turn.
6. Rewards, rating, plaque, footprint logbook.
7. Optional Simple Voice Chat (radio routing, push-to-talk).

## Questions to settle before starting (not decided)

- Is the tent required for alpha investigations only, or usable solo too?
- What exactly does it contain: sequencer, shared investigation board, sample chest, radio set?
- "One sends, the other writes it down": in what form? For example a two-screen mini-game, where the player in the
  field sees information that the player at the camp has to enter.
- What is the minimum number of players, and what happens if a player disconnects during an investigation?
- What is an alpha Pokémon here: bigger, higher level, guaranteed IVs, special moves, mandatory battle?
- Battles against Pokémon during the investigation: guardians around the clues, waves to fend off at the camp?
- Rewards: how to share them, and how to keep a passive player from getting as much as the others?
