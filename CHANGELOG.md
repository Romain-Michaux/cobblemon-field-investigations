# Changelog

## 1.2.0 (in progress)

### Case board
- The species' rarity no longer shows before a case is solved (it let players rule species out). Leads and the case
  sheet show a bounty instead, one to four stars: the rarity give or take one step, fixed per case. Rewards still
  follow the true rarity, which the "case solved" announcement now reveals.

### Mini-games
- **Abseil**: new art (climber, boulders, rock ledges growing out of the walls, gold-rimmed clues that glint), a short
  poetic intro instead of a key list, and the controls shown as key icons on one line under the game.
- **Bite**: a fossil jaw with real fangs over a putty cast in a steel tray; drag the cast with the mouse (a click
  presses it), the fangs sink into the putty when it fits, the jaw shakes and sparks fly where it jams. First round
  lights up the bumps that already match; the arrow buttons and the long text are gone.
- **Brush**: two soil layers (dry crust, then damp earth) over the whole width, a real brush that leans with the stroke
  and kicks up dust, and turns orange before a stroke gets too rough. The clue is broken into three jagged shards
  buried apart; covered shards sound hollow under the brush, two decoy pebbles sound dry. Freed shards lift with a
  gold rim, and the three fly back together into the whole clue at the end.
- **Cast**: a paw print in cross-section (four toes and the pad) filled from a jug that follows the pointer. The
  plaster falls for real, so it keeps landing after the click is released, and the stream thickens the longer it runs.
  One toe hides under a fold of mud and is filled by ear. An overflow spills over the rim and must be scraped; at the
  end the plaster sets and the whole cast comes out of the ground.
- **Centrifuge**: no more needle. The rotor sits in a steel housing with the sample tube in one bucket; braking makes
  it coast about a turn before it stops, so the tube must be stopped under the lid's window by anticipation. Each good
  stop separates one more layer in the test tube beside it (pellet, serum, pale band).
- **Chromatography**: now an identification. The sample strip climbs in a glass tank beside four, then five,
  reference strips; the sample is a blend of two references, to be named once the rack is lifted. Overlapping inks
  blend into a dull smear, each hue exists at two heights, and neighbouring inks only part late in the run: reading
  early is a guess, reading late risks the solvent reaching the top.
- **Climb**: a climber on the trunk, full width, keys or mouse. Grabs right on the hands are perfect and give a little
  momentum; double holds take both hands, vines are held while they pass. A miss cracks the branch and the climber
  slides down. The tree ends in a round bush with the trace on its last branch, scrolling with the holds.
- **Colour**: a bench with four dropper bottles and the flask; drops arc into it and the colour swirls. Colours now mix
  like paint (yellow and blue make green), drops cannot be taken back, and the hidden sample can be uncovered again by
  holding the click on it, from a small reserve. The verdict shows both colours and fills up to the score (88 % needed).
- **Count**: the dish lies in the dark and the mouse is a lens. Only one strain is counted, among look-alikes (a close
  colour, or the same colour in another shape); clicks pin colonies to keep track, and the count is set with the wheel
  or the arrows instead of picked among four numbers.
- **Crystals**: clusters on a rock wall instead of a grid. Sound ones are pried loose by holding the click; an unstable
  one cracks under the hand (let go in time) and, if it bursts, shatters its neighbours. Each deposit has a short time
  once the crystals go dull; rare sparks and shivers reward a sharp eye. Pried crystals fly into a vial.
- **Dive**: a lake scene over the whole width. The vial swims after the pointer with inertia, so the drifting trace has
  to be anticipated; air runs out in about eleven seconds, so the dive needs a breath or two at the surface (where the
  vial slowly loses some). Currents push the vial sideways, a fast pass near the bottom stirs up silt that hides the
  trace, and the trace no longer jumps when the vial fills.
- **Filter**: a spectrogram over the whole width, frequency across and time scrolling down. The cry is a bright line
  that drifts and calls in bursts; a fainter creature hums an unbroken line that crosses it now and then and fills
  nothing. The filter band follows the mouse and the wheel (or the arrows) narrows it: narrow isolates the cry faster
  and holds through gusts of noise but loses it easily. Once isolated, the noise clears around the cry's signature.
- **Fragments**: DNA pieces on a slate bench, dragged onto a rail behind a primer. A piece snaps under the end of the
  strand at the overlap the pointer gives, showing which bases match; it only holds when two or three bases cover the
  end exactly, otherwise it is thrown back and costs a mistake (three end the analysis). Tainted pieces hold nowhere
  and go in the biohazard bin. Three strands of four, five and six pieces against the clock; each finished strand
  pairs up, and the last one coils into a turning double helix that goes into a labelled sample tube.
- **Gel**: the bands only show under a UV lamp that follows the pointer, so lanes are compared a patch at a time, from
  memory, over the whole width of the tank. The difference gets finer over the three gels (a band added or missing,
  then its thickness or brightness, then a band shifted by one step). The verdict lights the whole gel with a size
  ladder, joins the sample's bands to the right lane, or rings the band that differs.
- **Measure**: three prints in the mud, measured with a calliper: set the fixed jaw on one edge, slide the other to the
  opposite one, across then along. The true edge is the hollow, not the ridge of mud around it, and a round loupe
  magnifies the spot under the pointer. Rain slowly blurs the edges away, a leaf hides part of the second print, and
  the tolerance tightens. Each measure is drawn as a dimension line; a field notebook sums up the three at the end.
  The print is the tracked creature's: its shape follows its first egg group (paw, three-toed claw, bird talon, webbed
  foot, insect tracks, bare foot or soft blob) and its size its height, so the notebook ends on an estimated height.
- **Microscope**: a round eyepiece over a larger slide, dragged like the stage, with a mini-map. The wheel turns the focus
  (shift for fine) and there is no gauge: the cells sharpen, and the sharp point drifts a little. About thirty cells,
  none quite alike, some with a lobed nucleus as a decoy; a card shows the anomaly to find (two nuclei, a torn
  membrane, an odd tint), sometimes on a deeper plane that only comes sharp at another focus. The right cell zooms in,
  stained, with a handwritten note, and its photo is pinned on a board.
- **Pairing**: a half ladder of DNA slides into a lit window where it zips up. Partner bases are typed ahead (A, T, C, G
  as printed on the player's keyboard) and wait, ghosted, under their bases; a blinking frame shows the next one. The
  strand repeats a motif, and damaged bases are guessed from it. No bar per base, one clock for the strand; a wrong base
  breaks a rung, which is mended while the strand stalls. The zipped strand coils into a helix read by the sequencer.
- **PCR**: a thermocycler driven by hand: hold to heat, release to cool, with a heater that keeps warming a little
  after release. Each cycle follows the real path (95° to separate, 55° to bind, 72° to copy), each band held a while
  on a live graph; a full cycle doubles the copies in the tube, and going past 99° burns a quarter of them. The end
  draws an amplification curve crossing its threshold, earlier with more copies.
- **Photo**: a painted forest clearing with bushes in depth, where a wary local Pokémon walks, dashes, comes close or
  slips behind a bush, facing the way it goes. The focus is set with the wheel and follows its distance, with a real
  blur when off. Three sharp shots are asked for; each click startles it off to a bush, and a viewfinder held on it too
  long makes it bolt. The light fades with grain. Each shot becomes a polaroid of the actual scene, and the three
  spread out at the end.
- **Pipette**: a close view of a lab bench: a micropipette with a wide graduated tip, a shallow dish of the strange
  deposit and a plate of three wells, each printed with its volume. Holding draws faster at first, and the liquid
  keeps rising a little after release, so the release comes before the yellow line. A missed dose goes back into the
  dish minus a little spoiled; a right one is carried over its well and pushed out, letting go before air blows a
  bubble. At the end the wells react one after the other and the plate is stamped.
- **Rockfall**: a rockfall blocking a lamplit mine gallery, built from one kind of rock, massive stones darker. The
  clue is hidden at its foot, its glow seeping through the gaps, stronger as the stones near it go. A stone comes out
  if the ones on it keep another support, and tumbles away in dust; one left on a single support wobbles, then drops
  into the hole below, which can block a way just cleared. Massive stones split under a held pickaxe. The freed clue
  rises into the light.
- **Sequencing**: a sequencer machine. Six bases to find, typed on the keyboard (or clicked) and run with Enter, each
  read spending one of six reagent vials. The read is drawn as a trace of coloured peaks, then each base comes back
  coloured: in place, present elsewhere, or absent. A notebook on the side panel greys out the bases each position can
  no longer be. Found, the clean trace scrolls and coils into a double helix, stamped.
- **Sieve**: a wooden sieve over a dig site, swung from side to side with the mouse. The sand sifts through onto a
  growing heap below; what stays in the sieve lags behind the swings, and a brusque one sends it over the rim, so
  the clue's fragments are seen sliding toward the edge. Clods are crumbled with a click, one of them holding a
  fragment. Things in the sieve push one another apart instead of piling up, and the fragments can be picked as soon
  as the thinning sand shows them (not the pebbles); they fit back into the clue, which goes into a labelled sample
  bag.
- **Sorting**: a nest seen from above, its hollow holding eggshells, twigs, pebbles and dry leaves, a bucket on the
  left and a sample box on the right. Things are flicked like marbles: pull back, a dotted line shows the path and the
  first knock (red when it would crack a shell), release. Only the shells with the card's pattern go into the box. A
  hard knock cracks a shell, a second breaks it; leaves hide what lies under them. The boxed pieces close up into an
  egg at the end.
- **Spectrum**: a spectrometer with a glowing screen and three real knobs (frequency, amplitude, phase) turned with the
  wheel, Shift for fine. The sample's curve shows through noise that clears as the measure builds up, and a lamp
  under each knob lights as that setting nears. The player validates with Space: 92 % match or more succeeds, less is
  a mistake. The validated curve comes out on a printed ticket, its peak marked, stamped.
- **Swab**: a close view of tree bark with an organic stain running down its furrows, and a cotton swab leaving a wet
  trail. Each swab holds a little over a third of the stain, bare bark filling it with dirt too, and a gauge shows how
  full it is; lifting it puts it away in its tube, and there are three, so none is to be wasted. The cotton takes the
  stain's colour. At the end the tubes are lifted and labelled.
- **Thaw**: a block of ice on a snowy clearing, a feather caught inside. A silent heat gun follows the mouse and melts
  the ice from its open faces, cell by cell, dripping into a growing puddle. The warmth lingers and spreads, so
  lingering above the feather cooks it (a gauge fills). Cold gusts, heralded by flakes from one side, refreeze that
  side's edge except where it is still warm. The freed feather is lifted out, dripping, and laid on a cloth.
- **Titration**: the pipetting lab's bench, a graduated burette on its stand above a flask on a magnetic stirrer. The
  tap opens with the wheel, from a slow drip to a running stream, and a click closes it; a drop hanging at the tip
  still falls. Near the endpoint each arrival leaves a pink swirl that lasts longer the closer it is, watched through
  a magnifier. A card gives the expected range and records each result; at the end it is stamped and the three pink
  flasks stand in a row.
- **Tracing**: three claw gouges torn in a trunk's bark, cast with silicone. The click held squeezes the cartridge;
  the paste lags the trigger, coming on and running on a moment. Too slow, it overflows onto the bark; too fast, the
  bead stays thin and is gone over again. The cartridge holds about half again what the gouges take. The set casts
  are peeled off and laid side by side.
- **Family tree**: the key is inked on parchment, with the sample's egg shell beside it. Its traits are no longer
  given: they are faint marks on the shell, read by holding a magnifier over them, among specks that are only
  scratches. Each fork has its own short time, so the traits are best read ahead. Guessing is allowed, a wrong branch
  is a mistake. Three lineages; the last is sealed.
- **Tweezers**: fibres woven into a nest's hollow, each with a free end and its tail tucked under a twig. A fibre is
  gripped by its free end and drawn out along its own line: pulled too fast or askew, the tension rises until it
  snaps, and where the tail passes under the twig only a gentle pull gets it through. Four of the five go into the
  sample bag, which is then sealed.
- First-time screens: every game's explanation is checked to fit above the Start button.

### Finding traces
- **Challenge traces**: one surface trace per case is earned instead of found. The radar leads to its start (a golden
  column); the player has three tries, and the trace appears at the end of the challenge (damaged, one dose fewer,
  after three failures). Nothing is placed or broken: the course is drawn with particles only that player sees.
- **The track**: a 200-block trail of glowing footprints sets off in one direction from the start, on the real ground
  (under foliage, around water). Tall beams mark a checkpoint every 30 blocks and the radar needle points at the next
  one. Each leg has its own countdown, sized for a sprint: run, jump the bumps, do not stop.
- **The tail**: a wisp drifts away along a path. It sees the player within 26 blocks when nothing stands in between: a
  spotting gauge fills, faster the closer, and drains behind cover, so the chase goes from one hiding place to the
  next. A ring on the ground shows its range and red motes run towards the player while it sees them. Full gauge, or
  more than 34 blocks away, and the try is lost; at the end of its path it settles and the trace is there.
- `/hunt challenge [track|tail]` (operators) turns the next trace of the current case into a challenge right in front
  of you.

## 1.1.0 (2026-11-12)

### Choosing and running a case
- **Pick your case**: the kit now reports up to three leads. Each shows the rarity of the species, where the zone is,
  whether you already caught that species, and what it rewards.
- **Ten kinds of special investigations**, each with its own rule and reward:
  - Anywhere: nocturnal (night only, shiny odds ×2), skittish (senses you from farther, double special loot),
    exceptional (one more result required, look-alike suspects, hidden ability), restless (moves twice as often,
    farther lair, double special loot).
  - By biome: aquatic (water species, traces on the bottom, target in the water), dig site (every remain buried),
    canopy (traces in the trees), highlands (traces on rock faces, wider zone), frost (one dose fewer per sample),
    mist (less precise bearings).
  - New loot tables: `investigation/special` for every special case, `investigation/kind_<kind>` for each kind.
  - Traces can now sit under water.
- **Team investigations**: the lead investigator invites players within 64 blocks from the kit's Team tab (search by
  name, usual partners listed first, up to `maxTeamSize` members, 4 by default). One shared case, shared stars,
  loot and experience for every member.
- **Cave traces**: one trace per case can sit in a shallow cave, 3 to 15 blocks below the surface, always reachable
  on foot from the outside. The kit radar then counts height and shows the distance and height difference
  ("In a cave: 23 m ▼8"). Aquatic and dig-site cases keep all their traces outside.
- **Buried clues**: a single one per regular case (dig sites keep three), dug up with the brush or the sieve, and what
  you find varies: bone or tooth, eggshells, feather or moult, fang, claw or scale.

### Mini-games
- **17 new mini-games** (32 in total), all with sound feedback and new pixel art:
  - Sampling: sieving, footprint cast, tweezers, trace photo, swabbing.
  - Analysis: microscope, chromatography, family tree, drop titration, bite cast.
  - Tied to the kind of case: free dive (aquatic), tree climb (canopy), abseil (highlands), UV lamp (nocturnal),
    thawing (frost), cry filter (mist, played before each bearing during the hunt), rockfall (cave traces).
  - Each trace and each marker now picks among several games.
- Several games reworked after playtests: tree climb (scrolling trunk, dead branches to let go by), plaster cast
  (tooth sockets, validated once the spout is lifted), bite cast, field photo (a random suspect as subject),
  sorting (drag and throw, objects push each other).
- **First-time help**: the first time a player meets a mini-game, its rules are shown and nothing starts before Start.
- `/hunt game [name]` (operators): open any mini-game as a dry run; without a name, lists them.

### Progression
- **Investigator rank**: experience for every solved case, seven ranks, and one kit upgrade per rank (sharper radar,
  spare vial, quick scan, one more forgiven failure, keener ear, shorter wait between cases).
- **Logbook and archives** in the Field Kit: rank and upgrades, closed cases, kinds of investigation already solved.
  Players who solved cases before this update start with 30 points per case.
- **Advancements**: nine of them, from the first sample to ten solved cases.

### Sound, interface, accessibility
- **Own sound effects**: 34 effects made for the mod (sieve, plaster, shutter, bubbles, heartbeat, rope, branches,
  stones, drops, scanner, success and failure jingles, rank-up fanfare) replace the borrowed Minecraft sounds.
  No music or ambience loop.
- Texts never get cut: long lines scroll inside their box.
- Case board: the tooltip is drawn above the item icons.
- **Accessibility**: per-player colour aid (colorimetry shows the match percentage while you mix); server setting
  `miniGameDifficulty` (`easy`, `normal`, `hard`) for the pace of every mini-game.

### Fixes
- Investigation state (cases, ranks, solved count) is now saved in each world. Before, every single-player world
  shared the same file in `config/`, so a new world started with the cases and rank of the others. A dedicated
  server takes over its old `config/cobblemon_field_investigations_state.json` once (kept as `.migrated`).

### Server settings
- Loot tables renamed in English: `traque/etoiles_N` → `investigation/stars_N`, `rarete_*` → `rarity_*`, `palier` → `milestone`.
  Data packs that overrode the 1.0 tables must rename their files.
- Config keys are now in English (`leadCount`, `maxTeamSize`…). An existing `config/cobblemon_field_investigations.json` is read as before and rewritten with the new names on first start.
- New: `leadCount` (1 to 3; 1 = no choice, as in 1.0), `leadValidityMinutes`, `specialCaseChance`,
  `maxTeamSize`, `miniGameDifficulty`.
